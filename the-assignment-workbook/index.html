About the book

Work book for the Assignment book! See what can happen when someone responds to the realization "the purpose with my life must be more than this..." This is His Story…

Horn of Africa, Upper Rift Valley—Most people have decided to get a haircut or a new pair of shoes on a whim. However, few people decide to change their whole life’s purpose, move to the Horn of Africa and tour the rest of the underdeveloped world in search of frontiers where no one has been before, simply because of a dream. Karl Hargestam did just that.

In his newest book release The Assignment: Journey to the Extreme, Hargestam bases the story on true events. Karl has delivered the message of Christ to more than 3.5 million people. Hargestam founded Joshua Campaign, a pioneer mission and evangelism movement that reaches hundreds of thousands of people each year who have not been introduced to the Gospel of Christ.

The Assignment: Journey to the Extreme follows fictional characters Cole and Monique as well as local tribal leaders and witch doctors, characters derived from actual encounters during mission expeditions. After a series of dreams and seemingly coincidental opportunities, Cole decides to make a mission trip! It becomes the beginning of a new life Adventure for their family. By connecting to the assignment God has for Cole, he experiences a new world of purpose and meaning opening before him.

“By following the created characters in their adventure through this book, I believe people will be challenged to search for their ‘assignment.’ There is no greater place to be! It is amazing can what happen if you respond to opportunities that cross your path,” says Hargestam.

What the book is about

The Assignment Workbook is a study guide written as a companion to the novel The Assignment: Journey to the Extreme. It is meant for individuals, small groups and classes, and it uses the novel’s characters and situations as examples for finding and carrying out your own assignment from God.

The guide has twelve lessons that follow the story chapter by chapter: “What Is The Assignment?”, “Called and Qualified”, “Favor in Captivity”, “Hearts Moved, History Makers Made”, “Opposition Has Arrived”, “Unsatisfied and Fruitless”, “The Prophet Arrives”, “Check Your Priorities”, “The Key to Unlocking Favor”, “Can You Hear the Voice?”, “The Great Temptation” and “Connect to Your Assignment”. Many draw on the books of Ezra and Haggai.

Each lesson opens with a quotation from a missionary or church leader and key Scriptures, then a short message. Then come Character Examples, questions about Cole, Monique, Randy, Mulu, Teguash and the others; Self-Observation, which turns the same questions on the reader’s own life; and Bible Knowledge. Short factoids add background on the unreached and the mission field.

Along the way it deals with feeling unqualified, opportunity and timing, opposition, sacrifice, priorities, hearing God’s voice and temptation, and it ends with a call to connect to your assignment.

Introduction

This study guide is designed as a companion for the first novel in “The Assignment” series, Journey to the Extreme.

It is intended to help individuals and classes use the novel’s characters and their situations as examples to apply to the assignment in their own lives.

The church, given many commandments and instructions in the word of God, recognizes that the only assignment given to it is to make disciples of all nations. According to God’s word, Jesus Christ will not return until the gospel is preached to all the world as a witness to all nations (Matt. 24:14). Every nation, tribe, people and language will be represented in the blood-washed church of Jesus Christ (Rev. 7:9).

Journey to the Extreme is based on real events and situations. The characters in the story are very representative of real-life personalities one might discover inside any ministry.

The story’s situations present real-life challenges as they might be for anyone rising to the challenge of carrying out God’s assignment in their lives. It is only when studying the hard realities of these challenges that we can best prepare for them. Like the novel, this Study Guide delves into issues with a fresh, honest introspection.

Each section of the Study Guide pinpoints moments in the chapters that serve as prime examples that will be useful to the reader. Examining the situation as it arose in the novel allows the student to learn the lessons beneficial to carrying out his or her personal assignment.

Students will be challenged by these examples to search their own lives and personalities and see not only how they’d react in a given situation but how they need to spiritually root themselves in ways they hadn’t thought of before.

It is encouraged for individuals to discuss the topics and share their thoughts and lessons with others. As shown in Journey to the Extreme, the path of an assignment often crosses another person’s similar journey and positive results are often reliant on the assistance of a fellow believer.

Lesson One: What Is The Assignment?

“No one has the right to hear the gospel twice, while there remains someone who has not heard it once.” – Oswald J. Smith

Scriptures

“And Jesus came and spoke unto them, saying, All authority has been given to me in heaven and on earth.” (Matthew 28:18)

“And this gospel of the kingdom will be preached in all the world as a witness to all nations; and then the end will come.” (Matthew 24:14)

“After, these things I looked, and behold, a great multitude, which no one could number, of all nations, tribes, peoples and tongues, standing before the throne, and before the Lamb, clothed with white robes, with palm branches in their hands.” (Revelation 7:9)

Message

At the core of the story in Journey to the Extreme is the belief that everyone has a right to hear the gospel at least once, and equally everyone has an “assignment” issued to them by God: to make disciples of all nations.

In the scriptures quoted above, we see how Jesus dictated that the gospel should be taught throughout the world. The only way for that to happen is through the individual efforts of believers.

Those efforts are generated at different times in people’s lives and under differing circumstances. Common lifetime obstacles must often be overcome before one can fulfill their assignment.

Beginning in Chapter 1, we see the characters of the story coping with everyday life. Too often, the demands of daily routines impede our spirituality and the characters reflect this.

Character Examples

Write answers out and discuss the following:

  1. What struggles is Cole having in Chapter 1?
  2. What are the challenges for Monique at the beginning of the story?
  3. Does Randy have an equal amount of obstacles?

Self-Observation

Having discussed the challenges presented for the characters of the story, take a look at your personal life and write out answers to the following:

  1. How do financial pressures like those Monique suffers in the first chapter of the story affect you?
  2. Do other people have misconceptions of you on the job the way Giles does about Cole?
  3. If people do have misconceptions about you, then how does that impact the way you project your faith on the job?
  4. Can people similar to Randy be found in your world of influence and do you see potential in them?
  5. In Cole’s position, how would you try to help a student like Randy?

Bible Knowledge

In Chapter 1, Cole repeatedly suffers from a mysterious dream that is trying to relay a message to him.

  1. What does Cole’s dream have in common with John’s vision in the scripture from Revelation 7 that is listed at the beginning of this section?
  2. Can you name at least two other references in the Bible where dreams were used by God to communicate with man?
  3. God ever spoken to you through a dream? If so, explain.
